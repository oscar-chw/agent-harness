# Research and scientific papers

Use when framing a study, assessing literature, designing comparisons, interpreting
results, drafting a scientific paper or responding to substantive peer review.
For a narrow editing request, preserve its requested scope and the author's meaning.

## Choose the relevant reference

- Start or redirect a project with [framing and literature](framing-literature.md).
- Plan or interpret evidence with [method, design and evaluation](method-design-eval.md).
- Draft or revise with [writing, figures and review](writing-figures-review.md).

Read the relevant part. Reuse existing project sources and decision records.
Resolve the actual question, contribution type, audience and authorized work before
turning an attractive idea into a larger research campaign.

## Working record

Keep the research question, competing explanations, decisive observation and intended
claim together. Connect each consequential choice to its reason and evidence.
Record source versions, exact read extent and locators. Distinguish personal advice,
venue-specific requirements, reported evidence and your own inferences.

For empirical claims, specify the comparison, independent unit, data boundaries,
resource budget, uncertainty and evidence that could change the plan. Preserve
negative findings and changes made after seeing results. For theory, retain the
assumptions, complete argument and known counterexamples.

Write claims to the scope established by the evidence. Link claims to actual
results or proof steps and retain unresolved limitations. Verify citations against
the source passages used. Research integrity includes accurate attribution,
faithful records, permissions, participant protection and honest disclosure.

Use the project's authorized verification process. Report what was inspected,
checked, reproduced and left uncertain. Scientific competence and publication
acceptance require independent domain judgment and evidence.
