# Writing, figures and review

Use this reference to turn the established work into a paper that readers can assess. The writing procedure is practical synthesis. Venue-specific requirements retain their named edition and track.

## Write to expose the argument

**Researcher advice.** Peyton Jones recommends writing early, isolating the central idea, making contributions refutable and listening to readers. A rough introduction can expose a missing comparison or an undefined claim while the design can still change. Draft the problem, contribution and evidence together, marking pending results explicitly. [SPJ, PDF pages 4–7, 12–24 and 41–44](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2016/07/How-to-write-a-great-research-paper.pdf).

**Practical inference.** Give each major claim a supporting result, proof step or source passage and a stated boundary. Write the abstract from the completed evidence. In the introduction, explain the problem, relevant prior knowledge, unresolved question and contribution. Methods should let an expert understand the comparison and its assumptions. Results should include the observations needed to assess the claim, including adverse outcomes. Discussion should explain interpretation, alternatives, limitations and consequences. Adapt section structure to the field and contribution.

Describe the closest prior work precisely enough that readers can assess novelty. Citation checking includes author identity, version, exact claim and relevant conditions. Cite original evidence for a result. Mark indirect attribution when the original is unavailable. A source's abstract and a later commentary may support different statements.

## Make figures answer scientific questions

**Researcher guidance.** Rougier, Droettboom and Bourne emphasize audience, message, medium and captions. Decide which comparison the figure should reveal. Use accurate scales, labelled quantities and units, legible marks, distinct encodings and captions that explain the sample, aggregation and uncertainty. Inspect the figure at its intended publication size. Retain data and plotting provenance so the figure can be checked. Their Figure 1 discussion also shows how a layout that communicates broadly can make precise comparisons difficult. [FIG, Rules 1–4 and Figure 1 discussion](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003833).

**Practical inference.** Distinguish conceptual diagrams and simulated illustrations from measured results. Give exact values where the argument depends on them. Define standard deviation, standard error or confidence intervals in each relevant caption. Keep uncertainty and adverse cases visible. Compare the rendered numbers with the analysis outputs before polishing visual style.

## Review as evidence about the argument

**Official venue guidance.** NeurIPS 2025 asks reviewers to assess technical quality, clarity, significance and originality, and to give actionable questions. Its originality guidance includes insights gained by evaluating existing methods. Use that edition as an example of a review lens and inspect the actual target venue's rules before submission. [NRG, Review Form](https://nips.cc/Conferences/2025/ReviewerGuidelines).

**Practical inference.** Ask a reader to reconstruct the contribution and strongest supporting evidence. Investigate mismatches with the intended argument. For each substantive review point, record the concern, evidence, decision and manuscript location. Fix an exposed flaw, clarify an ambiguous explanation, or justify a disagreement with evidence. Label any new analysis and revise all claims affected by it. Preserve the history of changed interpretations.

## Reproducibility and integrity

**Official venue guidance and requirements.** The inspected NeurIPS checklist asks for claims consistent with evidence, limitations, proof assumptions, reproducibility details and defined uncertainty. Its required checklist and context-dependent reproducibility provisions belong to that venue. Justified negative checklist answers can be acceptable. Verify the target edition, formatting, anonymity, authorship and AI-use rules at the time they matter. [NCP, inspected living checklist](https://neurips.cc/public/guides/PaperChecklist).

**Artifact example.** The ImageNetV2 README records named dataset revisions, curation and evaluation steps, duplicate review and distinct code/image licensing. Use it to understand which details connect a paper to an inspectable artifact. The README was read and pinned. Its commands and data were left unexecuted and unexamined in this drafting task. [IMGART, pinned README](https://github.com/modestyachts/ImageNetV2/blob/0b0c3fe89058e4c7ee2348938b3f4f706a931bf3/README.md).

**Official artifact criteria.** SIGCOMM 2026 distinguishes availability from evaluated functionality through different badge criteria. Its artifact packaging instructions request the accepted paper, artifact and exercise documentation. A reproduction report should state the exact paper version, inputs, environment, commands, expected outputs and observed discrepancies. Record whether another person or environment actually repeated the result. [SIG, Artifact Review and Badging and Packaging Artifacts](https://conferences.sigcomm.org/sigcomm/2026/artifacts/).

**Official definition and practical inference.** ORI defines fabrication, falsification and plagiarism in research records. Preserve actual observations, exclusions, failed attempts and attribution. Apply the project's relevant consent, ethics, licensing, privacy and disclosure requirements before collecting or releasing material. Keep authors responsible for the paper and verify every generated reference and factual claim. Record corrections where they change the scientific record. [ORI, definition](https://ori.hhs.gov/definition-research-misconduct).
